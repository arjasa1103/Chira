## Problem Statement

Build a data-mining and ML system that predicts sports match outcomes and reports
multiple risk categories with a win probability per category, benchmarked against
Polymarket prices. NBA first, generalizing to NHL.

The idea as originally stated contained a circularity trap: a Polymarket price *is* a
calibrated probability, so a model that consumes market prices and emits probabilities
mostly relearns the price. The project is reframed around that trap. Polymarket is the
**scoreboard**, not the feature set.

## What Makes This Cool

The artifact is not "a model that predicts basketball." It is an honest measurement
instrument pointed at an adversarial, real-money benchmark, reporting where it has edge,
where it does not, and why.

- **It is designed so it cannot quietly lie.** Pre-registration committed to git before
  the first model fit, a sealed holdout opened once, and parity as a success outcome.
  Most sports-ML portfolio projects are leakage artifacts with a suspiciously smooth
  equity curve.
- **It explains itself, with an honest caveat about what "it" can name.** Win probability
  decomposes into named, legible additive contributions. **Until the 2026-27 forward
  collector has run, those contributions are team strength, home advantage, rest,
  back-to-backs and travel — NOT individual players.** The player-level decomposition that
  originally motivated making interpretability a model-class veto depends on timestamped
  availability data that does not exist historically (see Blocker). This is a weaker
  selling point than "why 58.5% names nine players," and the document says so rather than
  letting the earlier framing stand.
- **Expected headline, stated before the work:** with the post-blocker feature set, a
  price-free team-strength + HCA + rest + travel logistic on NBA realistically lands near
  0.19-0.21 Brier against a market near 0.17-0.18. **Parity is therefore unlikely, and the
  expected headline is a quantified and explained 0.02-0.03 Brier gap, not parity.**
  Premise 5 already commits the design to surviving that; this states the prediction up
  front so the result cannot be narrated as a surprise.

## Constraints

- **Interpretability is a hard constraint that vetoes model classes.** From the user:
  parity is acceptable "as long as we have solid reason on why the odds is good." A
  gradient-boosted ensemble over 400 features scoring 0.18 Brier FAILS this spec; an
  additive hierarchical logistic decomposition at 0.20 PASSES it.
- Portfolio piece first, possible product later. No revenue requirement.
- Solo builder, free/public data only: nba_api, Polymarket Gamma + CLOB (unauthenticated).
- **Moneyline only.** Spreads and totals are explicitly out of scope; the verified slug
  format addresses a single game-winner market.

**On the evidentiary standard in this table.** Two rows rest on n=3 and are treated
differently on purpose. The complementarity row is marked verified because it tests an
*exact* invariant: "one derived series" predicts exactness, "two independent trade logs"
predicts near-misses, and three exact matches to four decimals at three different price
levels discriminates decisively. The liquidity rows are marked "recent window only"
because they are *frequency* claims, where n=3 in one window says nothing about November
2023. An earlier draft also listed "all three favorites won" as verification of closing-price
extraction; that was deleted as an overclaim, since three favorites winning has probability
~0.34 under a calibrated market and ~0.2 under a broken one and cannot discriminate.

**Gotchas that must be encoded in the ingestion layer:**

- **Build slugs from US-Eastern dates, taken from the schedule, not derived.** Use
  nba_api's `GAME_DATE` (and the NHL schedule's own `gameDate`) directly; never derive ET
  from `gameStartTime`. If you must convert, use the `America/New_York` zone with DST, not
  a fixed -5 offset, or March and early-November games break. (The probes behind this
  finding originally used a fixed -5 and landed on two EST dates; a follow-up probe with
  `zoneinfo("America/New_York")` confirmed the rule on 8 of 8 EDT-period October games.
  **Week-1 correction: that 8/8 result was real but the conclusion "always ET" was not.**
  Those games were all Oct 2025 NHL, and a wider 90-game probe found a mixed convention in
  Oct-Nov 2025. Emit both date candidates; see `chira.schedule.slug_candidates`.) A UTC-derived date misses the
  entire evening slate, which is most games: the highest-impact silent-undercounting bug
  available.
- `interval=max` with fine fidelity silently returns HTTP 200 + empty `history[]` for
  markets that ended long ago. Not data loss, a parameter artifact. Use `startTs`.
- `startTs`+`endTs` together → HTTP 400 `"interval is too long"`. Pass `startTs` alone.
- Gamma `/markets` silently caps `limit` at 100 AND returns nothing past `offset ~2100`.
  **You cannot enumerate a date window by paginating.** This produced two false
  "NBA markets don't exist" readings during the session. Enumerate from the schedule instead.
- Slug lookup requires `/events?slug=`. `/markets?slug=` returns `[]`.
- Pre-2022 (pre-CLOB) markets have zero price history at any fidelity.
- `/midpoint`, `/book`, `/spread` → `"No orderbook exists"` for resolved markets.
  Historical bid/ask is not retrievable retrospectively. `/trades` needs an API key.

## Pre-Registration (commit to git BEFORE the first model fit)

The git hash is the timestamp. Every threshold below is a number, not a promise to pick a
number later: a pre-registration with blanks is a template, and blanks are what let a
disappointing result get renarrated.

- **Sealed holdout:** the most recent full season in the census, opened exactly once after
  the model is frozen. ~1,230 NBA games before coverage losses, so plan on n ≈ 900-1,200,
  **not the n=500 the previous revision reasoned from.** At n=1,100 the paired-Brier
  standard error is ≈0.0007, giving a minimum detectable effect of ≈0.0015-0.002 at 80%
  power. Dev work happens on prior seasons only.
- **Primary metric:** Brier score. Log loss secondary, probabilities clipped to
  [0.01, 0.99]; unclipped log loss is unbounded and one mis-joined label at an extreme
  price would silently decide the nested test. No test is attached to log loss.
- **One primary test, everything else exploratory.** Family-wise error is otherwise
  uncontrolled across two nulls x two sports x two metrics x per-tier reporting. The
  primary test is: **Model B vs the recalibration null, NBA, Brier, alpha = 0.05.**
  Every other comparison is labeled exploratory in the writeup.
- **Calibration side convention:** home team only, one observation per game. The two tokens
  are exact complements, so plotting both double-counts every game and forces artificial
  symmetry about 0.5, hiding the favorite-longshot asymmetry the chart exists to show.
- **Nested test:** Clark-West MSPE-adjusted statistic. **Not Diebold-Mariano** — under the
  null the two nested forecasts are identical in population, the loss differential is
  degenerate, and DM's normal reference distribution does not apply. Specification:
  **fixed estimation scheme**, one estimation window on the dev seasons, a single pass over
  the sealed holdout. Critical values obtained by **block bootstrap on calendar date**
  (errors cluster by date and by team), with the analytic normal p-value reported alongside
  so the two can be compared. CW is derived for squared-error loss, which Brier is.
- **Integrity gate, as an EQUIVALENCE test so that imprecision fails.** The previous
  revision had this backwards: "CI contains 1.0" is accept-the-null testing, under which a
  wide CI from thin data passes and a precise estimate fails on a trivial miss. Corrected:
  - Market Brier within **[0.165, 0.195] for NBA**, **[0.205, 0.245] for NHL** (NHL sits
    nearer a coin flip, so its irreducible uncertainty term is larger).
  - Cox calibration slope CI lying **entirely within [0.90, 1.10]**, intercept CI
    **entirely within [-0.05, 0.05]**.
  - ECE point estimate **below 0.02**, with the bootstrap CI upper bound **below 0.03**.
  - Favorite-longshot tilt in the **named** direction — **longshots overpriced, favorites
    underpriced**, i.e. realized win rate below the price in the lowest bins and above it
    in the highest — with magnitude **under 0.03** in any bin.
  - Failing any of these means the pipeline is broken. Nothing downstream is trusted.
- **Risk tier boundaries, fixed now:** three tiers on `|model − market|` —
  **[0.02, 0.05), [0.05, 0.10), and ≥ 0.10**. Buckets are selected on the model's own
  disagreement with the market, so the top bucket is exactly where model error is largest
  and its apparent edge will regress out of sample. Per-bucket bootstrap intervals
  reported; expected shrinkage stated up front; unfixed boundaries would be a forking path.
- **Dev-side protocol (distinct from the sealed holdout).** Hyperpriors, feature set, and
  tier boundaries are chosen on the dev seasons by **rolling-origin evaluation**, refitting
  forward season by season. Clark-West is never computed on dev data. The freeze is a
  commit; after it, the holdout opens once.
- **Alternative closing constructions.** The nested test is run under **two** closing-price
  definitions: the home token's last `prices-history` value at or before `gameStartTime`,
  and a **trailing-15-minute volume-weighted price**. If B's gain exists only under the
  last-trade construction, it is measurement error in the benchmark, not market
  inefficiency. (Averaging `p_home` with `1 − p_away` is provably a no-op here, since the
  series are exact complements.)

## Premises

1. **The market price is banned from the headline model and required for the nested test.**
   Two models, two roles.
2. **Closing price = the home token's last `prices-history` value at or before
   `gameStartTime`.** Verified complementary (sums to 1.0000), so no normalization choice
   is needed. True bid/ask midpoint is unavailable retrospectively; flat runs are a
   staleness proxy — **flat run of ≥10 consecutive identical minute values immediately
   pre-tipoff flags a game as stale** — and stale games are reported as a sensitivity
   split, never silently dropped. The ingestion layer **asserts
   `abs(p_home + p_away - 1) < 1e-6` and fails loudly**, because a verified-on-3 invariant
   that deletes a design decision must be enforced, not trusted.
3. **Coverage exists; liquidity is verified only in a recent window; historical liquidity
   and density are unmeasured.** The census measures both.
4. **Success is calibration** (Brier, log loss) against market-implied probability — not
   accuracy, not ROI.
5. **Beating a $1.89M-liquidity market is unlikely and the design must survive that.**
6. **Risk tiers are edge buckets** (`|model − market|`) with pre-registered boundaries,
   each reported with frequency, hit rate, and calibration. An empty top tier is a valid
   finding. *This is a deliberate reinterpretation of the original "multiple risk
   categories" ask, confirmed with the user during the session: confidence buckets fill
   with obvious blowouts and carry no actionable information.*
7. **A portfolio piece must be legible without being run.** The deliverable is a written
   result with charts.

## Cross-Model Perspective

An independent subagent cold-read the session summary. Its disagreements were the value:

- **Premise 1 was wrong as a blanket rule (accepted, revised).** Circularity applies when
  price is a feature in a model claiming to forecast games. It does not apply to a nested
  test where the null model *already contains* the price. Model A: market price only.
  Model B: market price + features. If B beats A out of sample, the improvement is
  information model A did not already carry — though see Success Criteria for the
  interpretation ceiling: beating only the *identity* null is a recalibration finding about
  a public market tilt, not evidence of private information. The original ban forbade the
  project's sharpest claim,
  and made the only bad outcome read as failure; the nested framing turns it into a real
  finding about market efficiency.
- **Interpretability promoted from caveat to model-class veto (accepted).**
- **Draft analysis cannot be scored (accepted; corrected an earlier session error).**
  Futures resolve ~9 correlated labels per season. Four seasons gives under 40 effective
  observations. No trustworthy calibration score exists on that.
- **Best idea contributed:** plot the market's own calibration curve before any modeling.
  Benchmark line plus free pipeline integrity test.

## BLOCKER: point-in-time availability data does not exist historically

The flagship edge hypothesis was late injury/inactive news. **It is not testable on free
historical data.** nba_api's inactive list arrives in the box score, a post-game artifact
with no announcement timestamp. There is no free retrospective archive of NBA injury-report
publication times. Using actual inactives as a pre-game feature is exactly the leakage this
project claims it cannot commit, and point-in-time enforcement cannot save a source that
has no valid timestamp to enforce against.

**The same defect hits the NHL goalie feature**, which was the stated reason NHL is the
right second sport. Historical sources give who started, not when the start was announced
or confirmed, so the feature silently leaks late scratches and warmup changes.

**Resolution — split the work onto its two real timelines:**

- **(a) Forward-only availability experiment.** Start the pre-tipoff collector now,
  capturing timestamped availability and price snapshots. Scoped explicitly as a 2026-27
  season result, available around mid-2027. The 2026-27 season tips late October 2026.
  **Power, computed before committing a year of collection:** the subset with a late status
  change is plausibly 200-400 games, giving a paired-Brier SE near 0.0015-0.002 and an MDE
  near 0.004-0.005. That is plausibly above the true effect, so **this test may not be able
  to resolve even after a full season.** Pre-register it on the status-change subset with a
  stated minimum-n gate; if the season does not reach the gate, it is reported as
  collection-in-progress, never as evidence of market efficiency.
- **(b) Historical backtest uses only defensibly point-in-time features:** schedule, rest
  days, back-to-backs, travel distance, and prior-game results with known completion
  times. The writeup states the availability hypothesis and says plainly that it is not
  yet tested.
- **(c) The goalie feature belongs to the forward experiment, not the historical
  backtest.** The previous revision put a goalie sensitivity split in the historical model
  while (b) restricted historical features to schedule, rest, travel and prior results —
  there was no historical model containing a goalie feature to split on. Corrected: goalie
  identity enters only once the forward collector is capturing announcement timestamps. If
  a historical fit with actual starters is run anyway as a curiosity, the gap is
  **uninterpretable in sign** and must be labeled so — leakage can bias in either
  direction and the gap does not decompose into leakage plus signal, so it is NOT an
  upper bound on anything.

## Recommended Approach

**Approach B (full pipeline with forward capture), generalized across sports, with
team-BY-SEASON partial pooling (J≈90/96) as the transfer mechanism.**

Three things "transfer" could have meant:

- **Pipeline transfer (free, and most of the practical benefit).** Ingestion, slug
  construction, closing-price extraction, label joins, calibration scoring, edge bucketing
  are sport-agnostic. One `Sport` adapter interface; NBA and NHL are implementations.
- **Team-BY-SEASON partial pooling (CHOSEN).** Pool team effects within a sport at the
  **team-season** level: J = 60 for NBA and 64 for NHL across the **two usable seasons**
  (corrected from "≈90/≈96 across three seasons" — see Superseded note), with 82
  games per team-season, which is ample for pooling to pay. Sport enters as a fixed effect.
  *Revised twice. The first draft pooled at the sport level with J=2, where the hyper-SD is
  essentially unidentified, the posterior is driven by an unstated prior, and the fit
  collapses to approximately full or no pooling depending on that choice. The second
  revision fixed identifiability with static per-team effects (J=30/32) but treated a team
  as constant across three seasons — NBA and NHL rosters turn over substantially year to
  year, so a pooled three-year team effect averages three different teams and predicts any
  single game poorly.*
  **A random walk on team strength across seasons is MANDATORY, not optional.** The sealed
  holdout is the most recent full season, so that season's team-season intercepts cannot be
  estimated from dev data, and substituting the prior season's effect reintroduces the exact
  roster-turnover problem team-season indexing was introduced to fix. The random walk (or an
  explicit new-season prior drawn from the hyperprior) is the only mechanism that yields a
  forward-projected team-season state for an unopened season. **Decide and document how a
  holdout-season team effect is obtained without touching the holdout before writing any
  numpyro code.**
  **Pool only what has the data to support it: the team-season intercept (strength) and
  home advantage. Keep rest, back-to-back and travel GLOBAL** — NBA teams see roughly 13-15
  back-to-backs a season, so team-specific back-to-back coefficients shrink to the global
  mean and buy nothing while costing identifiability.
  A hyperprior sensitivity analysis is mandatory and reported, because on hierarchical
  variance parameters the prior can be the result.
- **Neural representation pretraining (REJECTED).** What the phrase usually means, and the
  wrong fit: it breaks the interpretability constraint, and ~1,300 rows per season does not
  reward representation pretraining. Overridable by the user.

**Parameter placement.** The **price-free headline model** carries pooled team-season
intercepts and pooled home advantage, plus **global** (unpooled) rest, back-to-back and
travel coefficients — see the pooling bullet above, which is authoritative on what is
pooled. Favorite-longshot correction and market-efficiency offset are defined relative to
a market price, so they belong **only to Model B** in the nested test. (Two earlier drafts
got this wrong in opposite directions: the first listed market-relative parameters as
pooled parameters of a model premise 1 declares price-free; the second left a stale clause
here pooling rest and back-to-back, contradicting the bullet above.)

**Stack:** Python 3.12 via uv. `requests` with on-disk response cache, rate limiter, and
resumable state. DuckDB. **numpyro** for the hierarchical logistic (not statsmodels, which
has no clean route to multiple partially pooled coefficients; `BinomialBayesMixedGLM` is
variational and limited). Budget real effort for non-centered parameterization, hyperprior
choice with sensitivity analysis, R-hat and divergence diagnostics, posterior predictive
checks. matplotlib. Notebooks render charts only, never logic.

**Dropped as YAGNI:** model registry. At ~3,700 rows per sport fitting one model per sport,
it is infrastructure theater. Rigor here is point-in-time correctness, a sealed holdout,
and pre-registration — none of which needs a registry.

**Point-in-time correctness, mechanically.** Every feature query takes a mandatory `as_of`
parameter; the feature-store API exposes no unqualified read path. A regression test
asserts a query with `as_of=T` returns byte-identical results against a DB truncated at T
and a DB containing all later rows.

**Data model.** Game key is nba_api `GAME_ID` joined to Polymarket `conditionId` via an
explicit join table keyed on (sport, ET date, away abbr, home abbr), because the
abbreviation mapping is irregular and must be auditable. Price table grain is one row per
token per minute: roughly 69M rows across three seasons and two sports, comfortable for
DuckDB but worth stating.

## Open Questions

1. **Coverage density per sport, NBA and NHL.** The census answers it. Early signal
   suggests NHL may be denser (116 vs 0 `nhl-`/`nba-` slugs in one window, 56 vs 3 in
   another), but both scans were truncated by the offset ceiling, so this is a hypothesis.
   **The census may reorder the sports.** Note the pooling design is now robust to this:
   team-season pooling happens *within* a sport at J≈90/96, so a thin second sport does not
   undermine the first. (The previous revision's contingency, "fit per-sport models side by
   side," was written against sport-level pooling and no longer follows.) What a thin
   result does change is which sport is primary, and whether Approach A is the right shape
   after all.
2. What exactly the complementary `prices-history` series represents. The two tokens sum to
   1.0000 and the final 8 pre-tipoff values were identical, consistent with a normalized
   mid-like construct with carry-forward. Determines how staleness is measured, and how
   much of the reviewer's measurement-error objection survives.
3. Abbreviation mapping, with a strategy rather than a hope. `sj` not `sjs` was confirmed
   from two NHL slugs, which means the map is **unknown for the other 31 teams**. Do not
   guess 4,100 slugs and interpret misses. **Learn the map first:** probe ~3 known games per
   team (32 teams x 3 ≈ 96 cheap requests per sport) to derive each team's abbreviation,
   then build the full slug set from the learned map. Remaining edge cases still needing
   classification: neutral-site and international games where "home" is ambiguous, play-in,
   NBA Cup. **Every miss must be classified "no market exists" vs "slug variant not tried"**
   or the census silently undercounts a third time.
4. Resolution pathologies, now with a handling rule: **games resolving `["0.5","0.5"]`
   (postponed/cancelled), unresolved markets, and UMA-disputed markets are excluded from
   all scoring and counted on an explicit census reconciliation line**, so the excluded
   count is visible rather than absorbed. Long-delayed resolutions are kept, since the
   label is still valid.
5. **Rate limits, unmeasured.** The census issues 20,000+ unauthenticated requests. Needs a
   conservative fixed delay, exponential backoff on 429, and a resumable cache so a block
   is not fatal.
6. Whether the availability hypothesis holds — **answerable only from 2026-27 forward
   data**, not from the historical backtest.

## Success Criteria

- **Headline deliverable:** the price-free model's out-of-sample Brier and log loss
  reported beside the market's, on the sealed holdout, with reliability diagrams for both.
  **Pass condition, consistent with the gap this document predicts up front: a deficit
  inside the pre-stated 0.02-0.03 Brier band, decomposed and explained, is a PASS. A
  materially wider gap is a finding about the feature set, not a bug.** (An earlier
  revision said only "parity is success," which the document's own prediction had already
  pre-declared a failure.)
- **Sharpest inferential claim:** the nested test under Clark-West. A null result is a
  publishable finding *if adequately powered*, and must be labeled underpowered otherwise.
  Report B against both nulls: the identity market price, and a fitted recalibration
  `a + b·logit(p)`. Winning only against the identity null is a recalibration finding
  about a public market tilt, not private information.
- **Interpretability gate, scoped to the final system** (not to the intermediate models of
  build steps 1-6): every probability the shipped system emits carries a human-readable
  decomposition. A number without a reason fails the spec regardless of Brier score.
- **Risk tiers:** each pre-registered edge bucket with frequency, hit rate, calibration,
  and intervals.
- **Transfer, graded on both halves.** (i) *Pipeline transfer:* the same pipeline produces
  NHL results with no diff in shared modules. (ii) *Statistical transfer:* pooled
  team-season effects reported with posterior intervals and an explicit shrinkage measure
  (posterior SD of the pooled estimate relative to the unpooled per-team-season estimate),
  plus the hyperprior sensitivity analysis. *The previous revision cut this second clause,
  which left the CHOSEN transfer mechanism graded by nothing — exactly the
  ungradeable-component problem this document uses to demote draft analysis.*
- **Integrity:** the market's own calibration curve clears the numeric thresholds in
  Pre-Registration. If it does not, the pipeline is broken and nothing downstream is
  trusted. Equal-count bins or a smooth LOESS curve with bootstrap bands, plus a Murphy
  decomposition of Brier into reliability, resolution, and uncertainty — which doubles as
  free explanatory material for the writeup.

## Reviewer Concerns (unresolved after 3 adversarial review rounds, 6/10 → 7/10 → 8/10)

Two items survived the review loop and need work before the model is written. Both are
recorded here rather than papered over.

1. **The integrity gate thresholds were set without a noise floor and will likely fail on a
   working pipeline.** This is the worst failure mode the gate has: a false "pipeline is
   broken" verdict that kills a correct project. The reviewer's arithmetic: with 10
   equal-count bins, per-bin binomial SE is `sqrt(0.25/n_bin)`, so ~0.05 at the holdout's
   ~100 per bin and ~0.027 at the census's ~350 per bin. Expected ECE under *perfect*
   calibration is therefore roughly 0.02-0.04 from sampling noise alone, which sits at or
   above the pre-registered 0.02 cap. The per-bin favorite-longshot cap of 0.03 is likewise
   below the per-bin sampling SE. The Cox slope window [0.90, 1.10] needs a CI half-width
   under 0.10, which plausibly fits at census scale (n≈3,500, SE 0.03-0.05) but cannot be
   met at holdout scale (n≈1,100, half-width 0.12-0.18) even with a perfect point estimate.
   **Required before the gate is used:** name the evaluation sample explicitly (it should be
   the full census, not the holdout), simulate the estimator's null distribution at that n
   and bin count, and re-express the ECE and tilt gates as *excess over the simulated noise
   floor* rather than raw point caps. Roughly a half-day of simulation. Until then the
   numbers in Pre-Registration are structurally correct (equivalence-framed) but numerically
   unvalidated.
2. **Clark-West block bootstrap has few blocks.** A single holdout season has only ~170
   distinct game dates. Report the block count, and consider a stationary bootstrap rather
   than fixed date blocks.

Closed during the loop and worth noting as resolved: the pooled-parameter contradiction, the
headline criterion that contradicted the document's own gap prediction, team-level vs
team-by-season terminology drift, the effort arithmetic that did not compose, the holdout
identifiability hole (random walk now mandatory), and the DST question (now verified in EDT).
